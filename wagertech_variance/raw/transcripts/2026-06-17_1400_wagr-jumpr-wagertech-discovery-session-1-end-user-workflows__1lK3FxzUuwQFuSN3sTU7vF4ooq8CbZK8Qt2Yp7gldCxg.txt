Summary
The team aligned on a desktop-first Salesforce architecture to streamline operations via centralized tracking and reporting.

CRM Strategy and Hierarchy
The team will build a desktop-first interface to optimize for the workforce’s current needs. Access levels will be structured to support operations, management, and Brand Ambassadors.

Data Tracking and Reconciliation
Manual logging errors necessitate a shift to automated Salesforce workflows to prevent revenue loss. The system must support gap analysis between forecasted and actual operator-confirmed conversion data.

Workflow Integration Requirements
The team aims to improve link attribution and deal management by integrating existing affiliate tracking into Salesforce. Standardizing these processes will reduce reliance on disparate manual spreadsheets.


Decisions
Aligned
* Desktop-first Salesforce development strategy The team agreed to prioritize building the Salesforce interface for desktop first, while ensuring the platform remains compatible with mobile use.
* Executive-level Salesforce access permissions Executive-level users will be granted read-only access to Salesforce.
* Workflow knowledge transfer strategy defined The team will conduct project discovery using recorded video demonstrations for workflow walkthroughs, followed by targeted meetings to address outstanding questions.
* ERD validation requirement established The project will require a validation session for the ERD to ensure the database solution is viable and prevents short-term project blockers.

Details
* Project Introduction and Team Setup: Kobi Kunasekaran introduced the discovery call for the Salesforce simplification project, establishing the team members and roles involved. Waleed Alasadi and Kobi Kunasekaran clarified project roles, noting that Kobi Kunasekaran will serve as the project manager and primary consultant, and Jester, also known as Mark, may join future calls (00:00:04).
* Role Descriptions and Responsibilities: Owen Scott and Jay described their roles in operations and sales, detailing their management of partner relationships, deal tracking in AirTable, and operational tasks. Ali Nawaz described their role as the head of sales, noting their experience as a Brand Ambassador and their ability to identify sales trends, which helps the team improve performance and address potential issues (00:02:22).
* Salesforce Proficiency and CRM Familiarity: The group discussed the team's level of experience with Salesforce. It was determined that most Brand Ambassadors have little to no previous experience with Salesforce or other CRM systems, which will necessitate user-friendly interface design and simplified training to avoid intimidation (00:06:25).
* Mobile Versus Desktop Usage Strategy: The team discussed whether to prioritize a mobile-first or desktop-first experience for Salesforce usage. While the team primarily uses mobile phones for daily tasks, logging is often split between mobile and desktop due to the complexity of the current Google Sheets process. The consensus is to focus on a desktop-first build that is also mobile-optimized, allowing for potential behavioral shifts toward mobile use as the UX improves (00:10:27).
* Organizational Hierarchy and Access: The team outlined the organizational structure for Salesforce access, identifying three main groups: operations staff, sales managers, and Brand Ambassadors. Brand Ambassadors are the frontline workers logging signups, while Brand Managers oversee these networks. The team confirmed that referrers will not have access to the system, but the structure must account for how numbers roll up from Brand Ambassadors to Brand Managers (00:13:22).
* Referral and Assistant Access Considerations: Joyce Kiddell and the team discussed whether sales assistants need their own Salesforce access. It was noted that while most assistants do not perform the logging themselves, having separate access might improve audit trails for error tracking, though this must be balanced against licensing costs and Salesforce limitations regarding read-only licenses (00:19:03).
* Access Permissions and User Counts: The team estimated the current user requirements, including approximately 22 Brand Ambassadors, 6 managers, and 10 operations staff members who require access. Kobi Kunasekaran emphasized the need to restrict access levels to prevent user errors, suggesting admin-level access for operations and view-only access for executive levels, with a proposal to be developed later (00:22:24).
* Deal Origination and Assignment Process: Jay and Ali Nawaz explained the deal management process, where Master Affiliates provide deal volumes (books). These opportunities are tracked in Notion and distributed to Brand Ambassadors based on performance, historical logging accuracy, and the ability to meet volume requirements (00:28:55).
* Player Value and Segmentation: Ali Nawaz described the strategy for assigning volume, which involves considering the quality of players. They look for players who demonstrate high activity levels, such as those making frequent deposits or wagering, rather than just high volume, ensuring that opportunities are matched to the best performers (00:32:55).
* Deal Structure and Categorization: Joyce Kiddell provided context on how deals are structured, explaining that they are defined by a combination of the operator, any intermediary partner, the vertical (sports vs. casino), and the market (geographic coverage). Future-proofing for potential additions like prediction markets and daily fantasy sports is a priority (00:34:43).
* Data Auditing and Metrics: Ali Nawaz explained that they audit player value and tracking by cross-referencing reports from operators—which include FTDs, registration dates, and deposit amounts—with the unique codes assigned to each Brand Ambassador’s network (00:38:01).
* Forecasted Versus Actual Data: Joyce Kiddell and the team discussed the importance of maintaining visibility into both forecasted (logged) data and actual (operator-confirmed) data. This gap analysis is essential because operator reporting often differs from what is logged, and having both sets of data allows for better performance monitoring and identification of discrepancies (00:39:59).
* Fraud Identification and Write-offs: The team addressed the need for audit trails to handle fraud and "write-offs," such as locked accounts or duplicate signups. By tracking these through specific reason codes in Salesforce, the team can better manage discrepancies between logged data and actual conversions (00:43:13).
* Operational Efficiency and Revenue Impact: Jay explained that manually logging data on spreadsheets often leads to missed logs and delays, which negatively impacts revenue and P&L. Moving to a Salesforce-driven process is viewed as critical to ensuring accurate, real-time logging and preventing revenue loss caused by missed or delayed reporting (00:46:35).
* Automation and Communication Future: The team discussed the potential for future phases to include Salesforce-driven automated outreach. Currently, communication relies heavily on WhatsApp, which creates a blind spot in the tracking process. By centralizing communication within Salesforce, the team aims to improve efficiency and enable automated reminders for deposits and wagering requirements, though they acknowledged this will require a transition process (00:51:28).
* Operational Challenges with CPA Logging: Ali Nawaz describes difficulties where BAs complete signups and registrations that qualify for CPA payouts but fail to log them in a timely manner, sometimes waiting until payment days or missing the opportunity entirely. Ali Nawaz notes that this behavior necessitates manual auditing of sheets and networks to identify inconsistencies, as they currently rely on manual updates from the BAs (00:58:19).
* Communication Strategy and Design: Emily Vandenberg and Waleed Alasadi discuss the need to improve communication, particularly regarding player notifications and surfacing signals to brand managers that require attention (00:59:16). Waleed Alasadi explains that while they do not expect to change player communication methods in the next three phases, they are considering utilizing "cases" as a tool for communication during the design phase (01:00:19).
* Reconciliation Process Demonstration: Kobi Kunasekaran requests a demonstration of the current reconciliation process, specifically regarding how decisions are made, which spreadsheets are opened, and how reports from operators match up against internal logs. Emily Vandenberg agrees to provide a recorded video to show this process in practice (01:02:16) (01:18:58).
* Data Management in AirTable: Owen Scott explains that AirTable is used for daily updates regarding book availability and assigned volume, as these variables change frequently. Ali Nawaz adds that AirTable is essential for organizing and linking specific BAs to operators, ensuring accurate tracking numbers are visible to the team (01:03:40).
* Operational Tracking Difficulties: Ali Nawaz discusses the challenge of switching operators for a brand in the middle of the month, which requires BAs to manually update logs to ensure accuracy. Ali Nawaz suggests that Salesforce could improve this workflow by automatically displaying the operator through which a client registered, thereby reducing manual logging errors (01:04:36).
* Link Management and Attribution: Joyce Kiddell clarifies that there are two distinct types of links: the BA-specific agency website link used for internal attribution and the affiliate tracking links that lead to operator landing pages (01:06:57). Ali Nawaz notes that from an operational standpoint, they assign specific affiliate links to individual BAs and may switch these links when targets are met, without the customer noticing any difference (01:08:03).
* VA Link Allocation Process: Kobi Kunasekaran inquires about how affiliate links are distributed to VAs, and Ali Nawaz explains that they manage a limited number of links by assigning them to BAs, sometimes rotating them to ensure high-volume reps are not duplicating efforts on the same link (01:09:10). Ali Nawaz states that AirTable is used to track the assignment and unassignment of these links to maintain organization (01:10:14).
* Signup Tracking and API Integration: Emily Vandenberg explains that current signup measurement relies on user clicks from the agency website, with a middleware redirect in place as the user moves toward conversion. Joyce Kiddell notes that the team is working toward server-to-server postback integrations with partners, though they anticipate only 50% to 60% coverage, necessitating continued reliance on manual reconciliation between logged and tracked data (01:12:57).
* Agency Website Scaling: Emily Vandenberg discusses the current structure of agency websites, which utilize "slugs" for each rep, and notes that the team plans to migrate to a single site for all markets before the project goes live (01:15:04). Kobi Kunasekaran emphasizes the need to see the signup flow in practice to determine which data fields can be captured and automatically mapped to Salesforce objects (01:16:08).
* Workflow Documentation Plan: Kobi Kunasekaran and Emily Vandenberg agree that the team will provide recorded videos demonstrating the top-down deal setup, signup processes, and logging workflows to help the design team understand the current operational reality (01:18:58).
* Data Analytics and Reporting: Kobi Kunasekaran requests access to executive-level reports to determine if they can be imported into Salesforce to support reconciliation. Joyce Kiddell confirms that the team has created mockups for an ideal reporting state and will share them via email (01:21:10).
